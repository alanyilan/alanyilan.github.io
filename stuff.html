Anqing

I was born in Anqing, a river city on the Yangtze, and spent seventeen years in Beijing before moving to the United States in 2011. That first leg turns out to be a well-worn route. In 1790 four opera troupes drawn largely from the Anqing region traveled to Beijing for the Qianlong Emperor's eightieth birthday, stayed on, and over the following decades their style became what the world now calls Peking opera. I made the same move about two hundred years late.

Anqing kept a form of its own. Huangmei opera began as songs women sang while picking tea in the hills outside the city and grew into one of China's five major operas — lighter than the Peking style, closer to folk song, and sung in something near the dialect I grew up hearing. If Chinese opera means painted faces and crashing gongs to you, Huangmei is the gentler way in.
